# Orbis: an atlas of Rome, 509 BC to AD 641

An interactive atlas, encyclopedia and narrative history of Rome from the first consuls to the death of Heraclius. Every region of the map has a year-by-year record of who held it and on what terms, so the Republic's slow absorption of Italy, the provinces won and lost, the client kingdoms, the breakaway empires of the third century, the successor kingdoms of the fifth and Justinian's reconquests all play out as you scrub or play the timeline.

Open `index.html` through any static web server (GitHub Pages works as is):

```
python3 -m http.server 8000
# then http://localhost:8000
```

## What is in it

- **Territory, year by year.** 385 map regions (modern admin units cut along the Ancient World Mapping Center's Roman frontier lines) each carry a control timeline from 509 BC to AD 641: Roman rule, allies and federates, client kingdoms, occupation, contested zones, and every rival state in its own colour, from the Volsci and Samnites to the Sasanians, Avars and the Rashidun caliphate.
- **Chronicle.** About 3,400 events with dates to the day where known, importance ratings, categories, ancient source references and notes on what is disputed; the major ones have long-form write-ups. Battles carry sides, commanders, strengths, losses and victors.
- **Battle diagrams.** 38 major battles drawn phase by phase, from Sentinum to the Yarmuk.
- **Wars, campaigns and journeys.** 159 wars, 107 campaign routes and 31 journeys (Paul, Hadrian, Egeria, Julian...) drawn on the map in their years, with a "Follow the march" animation.
- **Guided tours.** 12 narrated tours that move the map and timeline stop by stop.
- **Encyclopedia.** 1,340 people, 416 places (with Pleiades ids), 300+ states and peoples, 208 provinces, dioceses and prefectures, 65 legions with their bases over time, frontier works, named roads, 307 glossary terms.
- **3D Rome.** The city itself in three dimensions, changing year by year from 509 BC to AD 641 (the "3D Rome" button). It has:
  - The ancient ground, rebuilt from a 10 m bare-earth elevation model with 50 dated corrections: the Forum floor rising by metres, the Velia and the Capitoline-Quirinal saddle restored, Monte Testaccio growing, modern fill removed. The Tiber runs in its reconstructed channel at the water level of the period.
  - About 600 documented monuments in 907 dated versions. Each version is drawn from its measured size, orientation and form by a library of 35 parametric building types (temples by order and plan, basilicas, theatres, amphitheatres, circuses, baths, palaces as composed blocks). Buildings rise during construction and change through every fire and rebuild.
  - Walls phase by phase, from the Servian rampart to the Honorian doubling and Totila's breaches. Also 46 gates, 153 streets, the eleven aqueducts with their arcades, the bridges, the fourteen Augustan regions with the Regionary Catalogue, and the gardens.
  - 564 events pinned to the spot where they happened, from the geese on the Arx to the Pantheon's consecration, each with sources and a precision tag. Also 324 places of daily life (fire stations, markets, warehouses, mithraea, synagogues, tituli, catacombs, known houses), 25 processional and assault routes, and the burnt areas of 16 great fires.
  - Ordinary housing generated from researched urbanization zones (density and building type per period) along the known streets. It is labelled as generated wherever you click it.
  - Seven narrated tours, twelve viewpoints, a walk mode, a minimap, time of day, and jumps to the next or previous change.
- **The city on the map.** 279 dated monuments of Rome and Constantinople, with building footprints, on a faint modern street plan.
- **Coins.** 68 mints with their mintmarks (and a mintmark decoder), 44 famous hoards, monetary reforms, and collector notes on the coinage of every emperor and major usurper.
- **Society.** 469 economic, religious and cultural sites (bishoprics appear as Christianity spreads) and 35 trade routes.
- **Historia.** A continuous narrative history in 17 chapters, cross-linked to the map.
- **Fasti.** Consuls for every year to AD 541, emperors and usurpers, dictators and censors, and the dynasties Rome fought.
- **Fontes.** Which ancient authors cover any given year and whether their text survives.
- **Tabulae.** Charts: Roman-held land over time (computed from the atlas), coinage debasement, census figures, army size and more.
- **Tools.** Compare (overlay another year's Roman frontier), Quiz (guess the year from the map), life maps for people, battle density heatmap.

Keyboard: `Space` play/pause, `←`/`→` step a year (`Shift` for ten), `/` search, `l` layers, `Esc` close.

## Repository layout

```
index.html, css/, js/        the app (no build step; MapLibre GL and d3 are vendored in vendor/)
data/                        runtime data generated by tools/ (commit it)
data/geo/                    map geometry
data/fonts/                  SDF glyphs for map labels (Spectral), generated by tools/make_glyphs.py
data/city/                   3D city: city.json, terrain snapshots (gzip), generated housing, ground masks
js/city/                     the 3D engine (three.js, vendored in vendor/three/)
content/city/                authored city data, see content/city/SCHEMA_CITY.md
tools/build_city.py          terrain + city data (needs the TINITALY tile in ../srcdata/tin/tin.npy)
tools/build_fabric.py        generated housing and ground masks
content/                     authored source data (JSON), see content/SCHEMA.md
tools/fetch_sources.sh       downloads Natural Earth and AWMC geodata into ../srcdata
tools/build_geo.py           region cells, region polygons, basemap layers
tools/build_data.py          merges content/ into data/, validates references
```

The 3D city is rebuilt with `python3 tools/build_city.py terrain city && python3 tools/build_fabric.py`.

Rebuild after editing content:

```
bash tools/fetch_sources.sh          # once
python3 tools/build_geo.py           # cells, regions, base layers (needs shapely, pyproj, mapshaper)
python3 tools/build_data.py          # data/*.json plus validation report
```

## Accuracy

Content was compiled with AI assistance from standard scholarship and then given two fact-check sweeps; treat specific citation numbers as leads to verify. Territorial control is reconstructed region by region from standard scholarship and is necessarily approximate, above all for tribal peoples, the steppe, the early Republic (where the tradition is late and partly legendary) and fluid frontier zones. Early dates follow the conventional Varronian chronology. Items whose dates or facts are traditional or disputed are marked, and many entries say what is debated. Corrections are welcome: every fact lives in a readable JSON file under `content/`.

### Accuracy of the 3D city

Every 3D record carries a certainty tag. Of 907 versions, 384 are excavated, 100 documented, 167 reconstructed and 256 conjectural. Every building and event has been through a source fact-check (about 290 corrections in all). Positions come from excavation outlines in OpenStreetMap, the Pleiades gazetteer, published plans and measurements. Where a building's site or orientation is debated, the record says so and gives the alternatives. Superstructures above surviving remains are reconstructions in the generic forms of their type, not portraits of lost façades. Ordinary housing is generated, not documented.
