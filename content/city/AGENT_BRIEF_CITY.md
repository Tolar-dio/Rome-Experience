# Brief for city research agents

You are compiling data for a year-by-year 3D model of the city of Rome, 509 BC to AD 641, inside the static web app in `/home/claude/rome-experience` (an atlas called Orbis). The 3D engine renders whatever you write: a wrong coordinate puts a temple in the river, a wrong `rot` turns it backwards, a wrong height breaks the skyline. Accuracy of **position, orientation, size, height and dates** matters more than prose.

1. Read `content/city/SCHEMA_CITY.md` completely before writing anything.
2. Model area: lon 12.430 to 12.542, lat 41.850 to 41.935 (about 9 x 9 km around the Forum). Things outside can be skipped unless they cross into it (aqueducts, roads).
3. Coordinates. Get them from sources, not memory, wherever you can: the coordinates on English or Italian Wikipedia articles (fetch the article; the geohack link carries decimal lat/lon), Pleiades, the Digital Augustan Rome / Mapping Augustan Rome, Stanford Forma Urbis, published plans. Then sanity-check relative positions against what you know (the Temple of Saturn is at the NW foot of the Forum below the Tabularium; the Arch of Titus is on the Velia summit SE of the Forum; the Pantheon's portico faces north onto Piazza della Rotonda). Wikipedia coordinates sometimes mark a modern church or a street address rather than the ancient building's centre; correct for that when you know better. Give 6 decimals.
4. Orientation (`rot`): the compass bearing the façade faces. Derive it from plans and from the alignment of known remains (the Forum Romanum temples face roughly NW-SE along the square's axis; Campus Martius buildings mostly align to the north-south grid; the Colosseum's long axis runs ENE-WSW). State the derivation briefly in `note` when it is not obvious.
5. Dimensions in metres; Roman foot 0.296 m. Heights above the ground around the building. When a height is unknown, estimate it from proportions (Vitruvian column ratios, comparable buildings) and set `certainty` accordingly.
6. Versions: one record per physical version (fire, rebuild, enlargement, conversion to a church). Contiguous versions share `monument`. Chain them so `to` of one is the year before `from` of the next, or leave a gap if the site stood ruined. Ruins that persisted after 641 get `to: 9999`. Use `ruin` for a version that stood damaged.
7. Reuse ids. People and atlas events: grep `content/_existing_index.txt`. Existing 2D monument ids for the `monument` field: `content/pass2/monuments/rome_*.json` (read them; reuse their id when it is the same structure; invent clear snake_case ids otherwise).
8. Sources: real ancient references (author, work, book.chapter) and modern scholarship (Richardson NTDAR, LTUR, Coarelli, Claridge, Carandini's Atlas, Haselberger, Platner-Ashby with page numbers only when confident). Do not invent citations; omit page numbers you are unsure of.
9. Output: valid JSON only, UTF-8, no em dashes, each file under ~120 KB (split into `_1`, `_2`...). Validate each file with `python3 -c "import json,sys; json.load(open(sys.argv[1]))" FILE`. Write only inside your assigned folder under `content/city/`. Scratch scripts go in your own subfolder of `/tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/06b975e4-7772-558f-bf8e-2934a59ea71b/scratchpad/`. Do not edit any other file and do not run git.
10. Be dense. This is meant to be the most detailed reconstruction of the city available in a browser. Prefer many well-located records with honest certainty tags over a few lavish ones, but give the important buildings full params (column counts, podium heights, dome diameters).
11. When done, reply with a short report: files written, record counts, and the places where you were least sure.
